# CS-255

repository for CS 255 class with Professor Eppenger

The DriverPass project involved designing a system for a driving school owned by Liam, with Ian serving as the IT officer. DriverPass wanted a web-based system that would allow customers to register for accounts, purchase training packages, access online lessons and practice tests, and schedule driving appointments. The system also needed to support employees with scheduling, customer records, account management, and reporting while incorporating updated information from the DMV.

One part of this project I believe I did particularly well was translating the business requirements into UML diagrams. I was able to take the information from the interview transcript and organize it into visual representations of how the system would function. I particularly liked developing the activity diagrams because they helped me think through the different decisions and outcomes that could occur during a process.

If I could revise one part of my work, I would focus on the organization of my UML diagrams. Although they communicate the necessary information, some became more complicated than I originally anticipated. I would work on simplifying their layouts and improving the connections between elements so that someone reviewing them could follow the processes more easily without losing important details.

To interpret the users' needs, I reviewed the DriverPass interview transcript and identified what each person needed from the system. I then translated those needs into functional and nonfunctional requirements before incorporating them into the system design. For example, customers needed a way to schedule appointments, while employees needed to manage those appointments and ensure drivers and vehicles were available. Considering user needs is important because a system can function correctly from a technical standpoint and still fail to accomplish what the people using it actually need.

My approach to designing software involves understanding the problem before deciding how to solve it. I prefer breaking larger systems into smaller components, identifying how those components interact, and considering what could happen when something does not go as expected. In future projects, I would continue using requirements analysis, UML diagrams, and a combination of process and object modeling. I would also place greater emphasis on reviewing diagrams throughout development rather than waiting until the end, since even small changes can affect how different parts of a system interact.
